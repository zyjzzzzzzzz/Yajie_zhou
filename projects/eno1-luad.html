Ongoing project summary

ENO1-associated metabolic epithelial states in lung adenocarcinoma

This project investigates how ENO1-associated metabolic programs shape malignant epithelial states and spatial tumor ecosystem organization through integrative multi-omics analysis.

Research question

How do ENO1-associated metabolic programs contribute to malignant epithelial state transitions and tumor microenvironment remodeling in lung adenocarcinoma?

Computational strategy

  • Integrated TCGA-LUAD and independent validation cohorts to characterize ENO1-associated transcriptional programs.
  • Applied survival modeling, pathway analysis, and network-based approaches to identify biologically relevant metabolic patterns.
  • Integrated single-cell RNA-seq data (>33,000 cells) to resolve malignant epithelial states and cellular heterogeneity.
  • Applied spatial transcriptomics analysis, pseudotime inference, and CellChat-based interaction analysis to investigate tumor niches.
  • Performed RT-qPCR validation to support computational findings and generate experimentally testable hypotheses.

Key findings

  • ENO1 emerged as a central metabolic regulator associated with malignant epithelial states and metabolic reprogramming.
  • ENO1-high epithelial populations showed enrichment of glycolysis, hypoxia, and MYC-target-related programs.
  • Spatial analysis suggested that ENO1-associated malignant states occupy distinct tumor microenvironmental niches.
  • The integrated framework combined survival modeling (C-index 0.709; diagnostic AUC 0.906), single-cell analysis, and spatial inference to generate mechanistic hypotheses.

Biological interpretation and future directions

  • Current evidence remains primarily computational and requires mechanistic experimental validation.
  • Future analysis will strengthen malignant-cell identification using CNV-based inference and orthogonal validation approaches.
  • Spatial transcriptomics findings require further validation using approaches such as multiplex imaging or RNAscope.
  • Future experiments will investigate ENO1 perturbation effects on mitophagy flux, glycolysis, mitochondrial stress responses, and tumor–stromal interactions.
