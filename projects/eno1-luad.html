Ongoing project summary

ENO1-associated metabolic epithelial states in lung adenocarcinoma

A PI-readable summary of an ongoing cancer bioinformatics project integrating bulk transcriptomics, single-cell RNA-seq, spatial transcriptomics, and validation-oriented analysis.

Research question

How are ENO1-associated mitophagy and metabolic programs organized across malignant epithelial states and spatial tumor niches in lung adenocarcinoma?

Approach

  • Bulk RNA-seq survival modeling and external validation using public LUAD cohorts.
  • WGCNA and pathway integration to prioritize candidate hub genes.
  • scRNA-seq integration, epithelial subclustering, cell-state annotation, and pseudotime analysis.
  • Spatial transcriptomics analysis to evaluate ENO1-associated tumor niches and spatial co-localization.
  • CellChat-based ligand–receptor inference for hypothesis generation.
  • RT-qPCR and future functional experiments as validation-oriented next steps.

Key findings

  • ENO1 emerged as a candidate hub linking mitophagy-associated transcriptional programs and metabolic reprogramming.
  • ENO1-high expression was enriched in malignant epithelial states with glycolytic, hypoxic, and MYC-target features.
  • Spatial transcriptomics suggested co-localization between ENO1-high regions and specific epithelial tumor niches.
  • The project generated testable hypotheses about ENO1, tumor metabolism, mitophagy, and tumor–microenvironment remodeling.

Limitations and next steps

  • Current evidence is primarily computational and association-based.
  • Malignant epithelial annotation should be strengthened using CNV-based inference.
  • Spatial transcriptomics requires orthogonal validation such as multiplex IF or RNAscope.
  • CellChat and drug-sensitivity analyses should be interpreted as hypothesis-generating.
  • Future experiments could test ENO1 perturbation effects on mitophagy flux, glycolysis, mitochondrial stress, and tumor–stromal interactions.
