Manuscript under review

An ENO1-high malignant epithelial state in the spatial ecosystem of lung adenocarcinoma

Scientific question: Is the ENO1-high phenotype a tumor-intrinsic program, or a microenvironmentally sustained and potentially reversible malignant state?

I integrated bulk, single-cell, and spatial transcriptomic data to characterize an ENO1-high malignant epithelial population and examine its relationship with metabolic programs and the surrounding stromal niche.

  • Approach: analysis of 11 single-cell samples comprising more than 33,000 cells, epithelial subclustering, trajectory analysis, spatial deconvolution, cell–cell communication analysis, and targeted RT-qPCR.
  • Observation: ENO1-high epithelial cells were enriched for glycolytic, hypoxic, and MYC-associated programs and localized within a distinct epithelial–stromal niche.
  • Interpretation: the spatial restriction suggests that this phenotype may reflect more than a fixed tumor-intrinsic transcriptional program.
  • Unresolved question: whether lineage context or local nutrient and stromal signals actively stabilize the state, and whether it creates a targetable vulnerability during treatment.

My role: led study design, computational analysis, biological interpretation, targeted experimental follow-up, figure development, and manuscript drafting.

Published project

Tertiary lymphoid structure-associated immune ecosystems in lung adenocarcinoma

Research focus: To determine whether tertiary lymphoid structure-associated programs identify distinct immune ecosystems in lung adenocarcinoma and whether those ecosystems carry prognostic and translational information.

I constructed an integrated framework combining genetic evidence, multi-cohort modeling, immune profiling, single-cell analysis, and targeted experimental validation to characterize TLS-related heterogeneity.

  • Scale: three external cohorts and single-cell analysis of more than 118,000 cells.
  • Observation: TLS-related subtypes displayed distinct immune landscapes and clinical outcomes.
  • Biological insight: immune organization, rather than immune-cell abundance alone, may help explain clinically relevant heterogeneity.
  • Training value: this project established my interest in spatially organized tumor–immune ecosystems.

My role: led study design, computational analysis, experimental follow-up, biological interpretation, figure preparation, and manuscript writing.

Published project

BMP-related transcriptional programs in clear cell renal cell carcinoma

Research focus: To examine whether BMP-related transcriptional programs carry reproducible clinical and microenvironmental information in clear cell renal cell carcinoma.

This project used TCGA and external validation cohorts to construct a BMP-related mRNA prognostic model for KIRC, followed by analyses of immune infiltration, tumor mutation burden, predicted therapeutic-response patterns, and RT-qPCR validation.

  • Methods: prognostic modeling, external validation, immune microenvironment analysis, TMB analysis, predicted therapeutic-response analysis, and RT-qPCR.
  • Key finding: BMP-related transcriptional signals were associated with reproducible differences in clinical outcome and tumor immune features.
  • Training value: this project provided my foundation in external validation, reproducible workflow development, and cautious interpretation of prognostic models.

My role: led data analysis, biological interpretation, figure preparation, and manuscript drafting.

How I approach research

Principles for Rigorous Computational–Experimental Cancer Research

My previous projects trained me in end-to-end cancer bioinformatics and clarified where descriptive analyses must give way to stronger inference and functional testing. I aim to use computation to define precise biological hypotheses—not as an endpoint—and to evaluate those hypotheses with orthogonal data and experimentally relevant models.

Principles that guide my work

  • Use nested validation and explicit bias control when developing prognostic models.
  • Strengthen malignant-cell annotation with CNV-informed and reference-aware approaches.
  • Support spatial observations with spatial statistics and orthogonal validation.
  • Treat inferred ligand–receptor interactions as hypotheses rather than confirmed mechanisms.

PhD research interests

  • Single-cell and spatial omics for tumor microenvironment remodeling.
  • Metabolic tumor cell states and immune/stromal interactions.
  • Lineage and treatment context in malignant state transitions.
  • Functional testing of computationally identified dependencies.
