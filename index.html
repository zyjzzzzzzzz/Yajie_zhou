Ongoing manuscript · primary PhD application project

ENO1-associated metabolic epithelial states in lung adenocarcinoma

Research question: How are mitophagy-associated metabolic programs organized across malignant epithelial states and spatial tumor niches in LUAD?

I integrated bulk transcriptomics, machine-learning survival modeling, WGCNA, single-cell RNA-seq, spatial transcriptomics, pseudotime analysis, CellChat, RT-qPCR, and exploratory therapeutic analyses to investigate ENO1-associated mitophagy–metabolism programs and tumor microenvironment remodeling.

  • Key idea: ENO1 was prioritized as a candidate hub linking mitophagy-associated transcriptional programs and metabolic reprogramming.
  • Single-cell angle: ENO1 expression was mapped to epithelial states, especially an ENO1-high NAPSA-associated epithelial subtype.
  • Spatial angle: spatial transcriptomics suggested co-localization between ENO1-high regions and specific epithelial tumor niches.
  • Next step: ENO1 perturbation experiments, mitophagy flux assays, spatial protein validation, and stronger malignant-cell inference.

Methodological Reflection

My previous projects trained me in end-to-end cancer bioinformatics, but also made me aware of the limitations of correlation-driven biomarker discovery. I am particularly interested in improving the rigor of multi-omics studies through better survival-model validation, malignant-cell annotation, spatial-statistical analysis, and experimentally testable computational hypotheses.

What I want to improve

  • Nested validation and model-selection bias control in survival ML.
  • CNV-based malignant-cell inference in single-cell cancer datasets.
  • Spatial statistics and orthogonal validation for spatial transcriptomics.
  • Careful interpretation of inferred ligand–receptor communication.

PhD research interests

  • Single-cell and spatial omics for tumor microenvironment remodeling.
  • Metabolic tumor cell states and immune/stromal interactions.
  • Clinically grounded multi-omics modeling in precision oncology.
  • Computational hypotheses linked to experimental validation.
